THE MARGIN LEAK FINDER — the exact prompts, in order
Build It With AI — Episode 7
=====================================================

These are the three prompts used on camera, word for word. Two of them only
exist because the first build was wrong. That is normal. The checker
(checker.py, included) is how the wrongness was found — run it yourself.

-----------------------------------------------------
PROMPT 1 — THE SPEC
-----------------------------------------------------

I run a small candle company and I want to know which of my products still
make money after true costs, using the exports I already have. Build it as a
Google Sheets project: one script file of pure calculation, one thin file of
sheet plumbing, so the math can be tested outside Google.

GOAL
One report: for every product sold in the period, what it really earned
after every cost it causes — not just materials. Which products are leaking,
and what the leaks cost per year at this run rate.

DATA — three sheets I will fill myself:
1. "Sales Export", pasted straight from my store export, one row per order
   line: Date, Order ID, Product, Qty, Unit Price, Line Discount, Refunded.
   Assume the paste is messy: extra spaces, inconsistent capitals in product
   names, blank lines, the odd row from outside the period.
2. "Product Costs", one row per product: Product, Materials per unit, Labor
   minutes per unit, Packaging per unit, Shipping per unit (blank if never
   shipped).
3. "Settings": Card fee percent, Card fee per order, Labor rate per hour,
   Monthly overhead, Overhead allocation basis (REVENUE, UNITS or LABOR),
   Card fee on refunds (KEPT or RETURNED), Thin margin threshold percent,
   Minimum units to grade, Period start, Period end.

RULES — these carry the money:
- Prices are money. Do the arithmetic in whole cents with one written-down
  rounding rule, not in floating-point dollars.
- A product is what the export calls it after trimming spaces and ignoring
  case. "Sea Salt Jar" and "  sea salt jar " are the same product.
- The card fee belongs to the ORDER: the percent applies to what the
  customer was actually charged for the whole order, plus one per-order fee.
  Spread it over the order's lines in proportion to what each line charged,
  in whole cents that sum exactly.
- Overhead for the period is allocated over what was sold and kept, by the
  chosen basis.
- True profit per product = revenue kept, minus materials, labor, packaging,
  shipping, its share of card fees, and its share of overhead.

SHOW ME
A "Margin Report" sheet: per product — units, revenue kept, each cost,
true profit, true margin, and a verdict: LEAKING if it loses money, THIN if
the margin is under the threshold, EARNING otherwise. Worst first. Totals
row. A plain-English summary at the top: how many products leak and what
that costs per year.

-----------------------------------------------------
PROMPT 2 — THE FIX (written after the checker disagreed)
-----------------------------------------------------

An independent checker, written in a different language from your build,
disagrees with it on 86 of the 225 fields in my report. Every disagreement
traces to two decisions you made:

1. You drop refunded lines at the door, as if the sale never happened. A
   refund is not a disappearance. The customer's money went back, but the
   materials, labor, packaging and shipping were already spent, and the
   processor keeps its fee when the settings say KEPT. Refunds are a cost of
   the product that caused them. Give every product a refund-cost column:
   the spent unit costs of its refunded units plus their share of the card
   fee, and keep refunded revenue out of revenue kept.

2. You charge the per-order card fee on every LINE. A three-line order pays
   the per-order fee once, not three times. Compute the fee per ORDER on the
   order's charged total, then split it across the order's lines in
   proportion to what each line charged, in whole cents that sum exactly.

Do not tell me it is fixed. Re-run the comparison and show me zero
disagreements, on my export and on the generated businesses.

-----------------------------------------------------
PROMPT 3 — THE MONEY THAT VANISHED
-----------------------------------------------------

$2,831.50 of my export never reaches the report, and nothing on the report
says so. The gift cards and the workshop tickets have no cost rows, so you
skip those lines silently. A report that quietly drops revenue is how margin
leaks hide in the first place — the tool is doing the thing it exists to
catch.

Change it so the report accounts for every charged dollar in the export:
- Every line the report cannot use is listed at the bottom with its sheet
  row and the reason, and its revenue is counted.
- Product Costs gets an IGNORE flag for lines that are real revenue but not
  products — gift cards, tickets. Ignored revenue is shown, not graded.
- A reconciliation line ties the report back to the export to the cent, and
  says TIES OUT or DOES NOT TIE OUT.
- While the unmatched revenue is more than half a percent of the export, the
  report prints PROVISIONAL and withholds every verdict. A wrong grade is
  worse than no grade.
- And a product with fewer kept units than the minimum in Settings gets
  LOW DATA instead of a verdict — two sales is an anecdote, not a margin.
